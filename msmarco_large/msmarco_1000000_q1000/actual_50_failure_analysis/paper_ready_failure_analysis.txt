We analyzed 50 actual IR-unsafe query-budget cases using a structured model-assisted taxonomy. Cases initially flagged as uncertain were resolved through a second evidence-based model-assisted review using predefined numerical and semantic decision rules. These cases have nDCG@10 loss greater than 0.025 and were deliberately selected from the HNSW budget sweep; they are not 50 failures of the final CURA operating policy. Of these cases, 16 (32%) have ANN Recall@10 >= 0.90, 34 have lower recall, 19 have an exact rank-10/11 gap <= 0.001, and 8 are TREC-DL-2019 cases. The dominant primary labels are NEAR_TIE_BOUNDARY (17/50), HIGH_ANN_RECALL_BUT_IR_FAILURE (16/50), LOW_ANN_RECALL_AND_IR_FAILURE (9/50). Incomplete ANN recovery is common, but high ANN overlap also coexists with substantial downstream utility loss. The small boundary is an observed feature rather than proof of which relevant documents were missed. This deliberately enriched sample cannot estimate population failure prevalence or demonstrate a CURA-policy advantage. All 50 final labels are model-assisted, including the eight automatically resolved cases; no cases were human verified in this finalization.
